01 / The problem

Big Data changes the search problem

Information volume is growing faster than organizations can evaluate it. The challenge is not only how much data exists, but whether heterogeneous data can be interpreted as one meaningful system.

Quantity

Large amounts of data require performance corresponding to the time of relevance.

≈ 40% annual growth of stored online data

Quality

Heterogeneous syntax requires normalization to identify semantics. Decision maker, logical operations, Big Data and network are divided into quantity and quality problems.

02 / The model

Feedback turns uncertainty into a measurable process

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

What is an I-equivalent in simple terms? +

It is a compressed mathematical representation of data that makes completely different, unstructured information sources easily comparable by bringing them to a single coherent metric format.

How does annual growth of Big Data affect business search efficiency? +

With data volume growing at roughly 40% per year, traditional brute-force index searches fail to verify quality or relevance in real-time, resulting in uncalculated operational risk indicators.

What is the purpose of the forward and return loops? +

The forward loop strips analytical uncertainty from the initial request by scaling down descriptors. The return loop feeds structured analytical telemetry back into the manager's metrics framework.

Information retrieval becomes a feedback-driven I-equivalent process

The approach makes it possible to design business technologies with specific software and hardware solutions that optimize data collection, processing and analysis.
